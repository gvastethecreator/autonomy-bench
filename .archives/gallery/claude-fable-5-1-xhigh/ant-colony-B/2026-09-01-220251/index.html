    How the colony finds its way

    Every ant here follows the same three rules. It walks, wobbling a little. It smells the ground at three points just ahead and turns towards the strongest scent. And it lays scent of its own: a home scent while searching, which weakens the further it has walked from the nest, and a food scent while carrying, which weakens the further it has walked from the food.

    Scent evaporates. A route stays fresh only while ants keep walking it, and a short route is walked more often than a long one, so the colony converges on efficient paths without any single ant knowing the map. Biologists call this stigmergy: coordination through traces left in the environment.

    Things to try: drop food far from the nest and watch a trail form; lay a stone across a busy trail; call rain to wash every trail away and watch the colony recover. Food feeds the queen, and the colony grows or shrinks with what it brings home.

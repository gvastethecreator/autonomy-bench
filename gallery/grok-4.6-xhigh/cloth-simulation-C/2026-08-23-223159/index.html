Study for a curtain

Each crossing in the weave is a mass. Neighbouring masses pull toward a rest length — that is the cloth. A weaker pull two crossings away keeps folds from collapsing into paper. Wind presses on each face in proportion to how much it presents to the air.

The silk is shot: the warp reads crimson, the weft teal. Turn a fold and the colour slips. Light that makes it through stains the plinth.

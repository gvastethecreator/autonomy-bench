Crowd evacuation

People walk toward the nearest open exit, push off walls and each other, and jam when the door is too small. Sound the alarm, paint a fire, or narrow the exits and watch the building empty — or fail to.

Color is stress, not team. Waiting people stay muted until the alarm. Fire blocks the flow field, so the crowd reroutes if another exit still exists. Raise desired speed on a narrow door to see the faster-is-slower jam: more pushing, a tighter arch, a slower empty building.

How the model moves people

Each person has a desired velocity taken from a multi-source distance field to every walkable exit. That field is Dijkstra on a 0.4 m grid, 8-connected, with no diagonal corner cutting. Fire above a burn threshold and barricades are walls for pathfinding, so a blocked door reroutes the crowd if another exit remains.

Motion is a social-force model in the Helbing–Farkas–Vicsek family. A relaxation term steers velocity toward the desired heading. Nearby bodies add an exponential repulsive force; overlap adds a stiff compression force and sliding friction. Walls use a distance field with the same shape. A little heading noise breaks perfectly symmetric arches so a jam can collapse.

Herding blends a person’s desired heading with neighbors’ velocity. Pre-movement draws a delay after the alarm before that person starts to go. Smoke cuts speed; high heat incapacitates. The famous faster-is-slower effect is not scripted — it appears when high desired speed piles bodies into a door and friction dominates.
